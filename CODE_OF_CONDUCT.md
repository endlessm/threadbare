# Threadbare Code of Conduct

## The short version

Threadbare is built in the open by learners, mentors, contributors, and the Endless Access team, and everyone who takes part follows this Code of Conduct.

Do

- Be kind and welcoming, especially to people who are new here.
- Disagree with ideas, not with people.
- Give feedback that helps, and be open to feedback on your own work.
- Keep conversations in the right place: the right channel, issue, or pull request.

Don't

- Insult, bully, troll, or harass anyone, for any reason.
- Post anything sexual, violent, or disturbing, like self-harm, hate symbols, or shock content. That includes usernames, profile pictures, and links.
- Share anyone's personal information, or ask for theirs.

If something feels wrong, report it. Don't fight back. Email community@endlessaccess.org or message a moderator. If anyone asks you to keep a secret or to talk somewhere private, tell us. Every report is kept private and looked into.

The rest of this page explains each part in more detail.

## Our commitment

We want Threadbare to be safe and welcoming for everyone who plays, learns, or contributes. Nobody here should face harassment.

### Where this applies

This Code of Conduct covers everyone who takes part in Threadbare, wherever that happens:

- This repository, including issues, pull requests, code reviews, discussions, and the wiki
- The Endless Access Discord
- Endless Access programs and events where people work on Threadbare
- Playtests and livestreams
- Anyone representing Threadbare or Endless Access in public, on social media or at events

## What we expect from everyone

Be respectful. Value other people's ideas, styles, and points of view. You won't always agree, and that's fine. Disagreeing is never an excuse to be rude.

Be direct and kind. Say what you think clearly. In reviews and comments, focus on the work, not the person, and suggest how to make it better.

Be inclusive. Welcome newcomers and first-time contributors. Make room for quieter voices, people whose first language is different from yours, and people joining from other time zones. If you notice you're doing most of the talking, step back.

Learn from each other. The point of a disagreement isn't to win. It's to end up with a better idea. Be open to being wrong, and fail forward: every mistake is a chance to learn and get better.

Lead by example. Maintainers, mentors, moderators, and staff are held to these standards first. The more responsibility you have, the more this applies to you.

## What's not okay

These behaviors aren't allowed anywhere in our community, in public channels or in private messages.

- Harassment and bullying. Targeting someone, following them around, or contacting them after they've asked you to stop.
- Personal attacks and hurtful language. Insults, trolling, slurs, or putting people down. If you're not sure a word is hurtful, don't use it.
- Sexual content or attention. Nudity, sexual jokes, language, or images, and sexual advances of any kind. This covers usernames, nicknames, profile pictures, and bios too.
- Violence and threats. Threatening anyone, encouraging someone to hurt themselves, or posting violent or disturbing content.
- Sharing private information. Posting or asking for someone's real name, address, phone number, school, photos, or voice without their permission.

### Keeping young people safe

Here, "young people" means anyone 21 or under. Older teens and young adults can be just as vulnerable as younger learners, so these rules protect all of them.

- No one should ask a young person to keep a conversation secret, move to a private chat, or connect on another app.
- Mentors, volunteers, and staff keep their communication with learners in official Endless Access spaces.
- Any concern about a young person's safety is treated as urgent.

## How to report

If something doesn't feel right, report it, whether it's happening to you or to someone else. Don't argue back or try to handle it alone.

- Email community@endlessaccess.org
- On GitHub, email us a link to the issue, pull request, or comment
- On Discord, message a moderator or admin
- At a program or event, tell the mentor or organizer running it

Tell us what happened, where and when, and who was involved. Screenshots or links help if you have them.

What we promise. We keep the identity of the person reporting private. We review every report fairly and look into it properly, and we'll confirm that we received it.

## What happens after a report

Moderators choose a response based on what happened, how it affected people, and any past behavior. A serious incident can go straight to a higher step.

| Step | When it applies | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Prevention | Content that could put the community at immediate risk | Automated tools or moderators hide or remove it, ideally before anyone sees it |
| 1. Correction | Inappropriate or unwelcome language or behavior | A private written note explaining what went wrong and why. We may ask for an apology. |
| 2. Warning | A single serious incident, or a pattern of smaller ones | A formal warning with consequences if it continues, and no contact with the people involved for a set time |
| 3. Temporary ban | A serious violation, or ongoing inappropriate behavior | Removed from all community spaces for a set time |
| 4. Permanent ban | A pattern of violations, harassing someone, or targeting groups of people | Removed from the community for good |

Anyone asked to stop a behavior is expected to stop right away. Moderators can also remove or edit messages, code, issues, or other content that breaks these rules, and will explain why when appropriate.

These rules apply to everyone, including maintainers, mentors, moderators, and staff. Staff and contractors may also face consequences under their work agreements.

If you disagree with a decision, you can appeal by emailing learning@endlessaccess.org.
